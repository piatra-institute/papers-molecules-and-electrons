# Brief

## Question

A Bucharest hypothesis holds that Romania's gas turn (under the presidency and AUR alike) serves a US "energy dominance" strategy to keep Europe on molecules and out of an autonomous electron economy, pricing the stake as a 1.7-3.6% hit to the US current account. Is the stake real, and if so, what kind of object is it?

## Claim

The intuition is right and the arithmetic is wrong. "Energy power" fuses two forms that behave oppositely: molecule power is power over a recurring flow metered at a chokepoint (rent recurs, leverage is a tap); electron power is power over a stock and a standard (capital bought once, fuel then free, dependence is lock-in). Molecule revenue requires the customer to stay un-electrified, so a fossil exporter has a structural interest in delay. In a bistable replicator-with-learning model of an importer's electric share, the exporter's price moves the importer's takeoff threshold, producing a harvest-vs-extend dilemma: limit-pricing (suppressing one's own price to trap the importer) beats harvesting only when the exporter is patient and substitution is fast, so choosing it is a tell that the actor believes takeoff is near. The current-account channel is ~0.13-0.23% (order of magnitude below the claim), but the real stake is the capitalized European rent a takeoff terminates (~$289bn, ~113x the annual figure) in a different ledger. The delay is bounded because the learning curve is global; the Romanian outcome is overdetermined by independent optima, so coordination is unnecessary, not merely unproven.
