## Findings

- [T1] Trade dependence is leverage: the power one state holds over another is the power to interrupt a flow the other depends on, largest where the dependence is hard to redirect — Hirschman (1945), *National Power and the Structure of Foreign Trade*. Grounds molecule power as chokepoint power (§2).
- [T4] The petrostate/electrostate contrast as ideal types of how a state converts energy into geopolitical power — Tooze (2025), Chartbook 439. The framing the paper sharpens; cited as the origin of the molecule/electron vocabulary (§2). Treated as a lead, not authority.
- [T2] Geopolitics of renewables differs from oil/gas because the tradeable object is equipment, grids, and standards, not a fuel flow — Scholten and Bosman (2016), *Technological Forecasting and Social Change*; Scholten (2018), *The Geopolitics of Renewables*; Overland et al. (2019), GeGaLo index, *Energy Strategy Reviews*. Grounds electron power as stock/standard power (§2).
- [T2] Carbon lock-in: technology, institutions, and infrastructure co-evolve into a self-reinforcing fossil equilibrium — Unruh (2000), *Energy Policy* 28(12):817-830. The bistability of the model stated qualitatively (§3). Path-dependence mechanism: Arthur (1989), *Economic Journal*; David (1985), *AER*.
- [T1] Directed technical change: policy can redirect innovation toward clean sectors, after which relative costs evolve endogenously, creating tipping behavior — Acemoglu, Aghion, Bursztyn and Hemous (2012), *AER* 102(1):131-166. Grounds the learning term in Δ(x) and the stickiness of the threshold (§3).
- [T1] Experience/learning curves: unit cost falls a roughly fixed fraction per doubling of cumulative deployment, holding more reliably for clean tech than fossil extraction — Nagy, Farmer, Bui and Trancik (2013), *PLoS ONE*; Way, Ives, Mealy and Farmer (2022), *Joule* 6(9):2057-2082. Grounds the learning slope ℓ and the global, rival-driven cost decline (§3, §7).
- [T3] Replicator dynamics as the canonical model of competition/adoption between two systems — Hofbauer and Sigmund (1998), *Evolutionary Games and Population Dynamics*. The dynamical form ẋ = ηx(1-x)Δ(x) (§3).
- [T2] Energy security and the regulatory/strategic state in energy markets — Yergin (2011), *The Quest*; Goldthau and Sitter (2015), *A Liberal Actor in a Realist World*. Background for "geopolitics of taps" (§2).
- [T1] US current-account deficit ~$1.12 trillion in 2025 (3.6% of GDP) — BEA, U.S. International Transactions, Q4 and year 2025. Anchors the magnitude repair (§5); corrects the hypothesis's $700-900bn assumption.
- [T1] US LNG export volumes and prices (2025): ~5.5 Tcf global at ~$7.92/mcf; EU destinations ~86 bcm — EIA natural gas exports by country. Anchors the rent/volume accounting and the 5-9 bcm displacement value (§4, §5).
- [T1] Electrification shares of final energy: ~28% China, ~22% US, ~21% EU — IEA, Electricity 2025. Sets x0 = 0.21 and establishes the transition is early (§2, §3).
- [T4] Neptun Deep ~8 bcm/yr from 2027, roughly doubling Romanian output and making it a net exporter; AUR demands for new gas generation; the US "energy dominance" / LNG-leadership framing — Romanian Presidency, AUR, US DOE public materials (per the seed). Used in §6 as illustration; the 8 bcm competing with US LNG is the structural-complementarity-with-rivalry point. No quantitative claim is attributed to these.
- [T2] An importer electrifying on another power's equipment swaps fuel dependence for industrial dependence and is not thereby sovereign; the EU Net-Zero Industry Act responds to this — EU Commission materials (per the seed). The honesty caveat in §2.
